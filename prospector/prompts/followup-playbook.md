**What a follow-up is** — a short reply in the same thread, sent because the first
email got no answer. It lands right below the earlier email(s), so the reader can
scroll down and see who you are and what you offered: don't repeat any of it.

**Shape** — as natural prose (no headers or labels):

1. **Greeting** — the same short greeting as before ("Buenas,").
2. **The thread back** — one sentence that points at the earlier email by the one
   task it named ("Os escribí hace unos días sobre el tiempo que se va en
   reformatear CVs."). Never "just following up", never a guilt trip.
3. **One new thing** — a single sentence or two that give the reader a reason to
   answer now, grounded in the research: a concrete picture of what the agent
   would do with that same task in their day ("el agente recibe el CV, lo pasa a
   la plantilla del cliente y os lo deja listo para revisar"), or a different
   angle on the same pain. Same task as the first email — never a new pitch or a
   second task.
4. **The ask** — one light question proposing the same 20-minute call, in your
   own words this time ("¿Os encaja verlo en 20 minutos esta semana o la
   siguiente?"), followed by a courteous easy out.
5. **Valediction** ("Un saludo,") above the signature.

**If it's a second (or later) follow-up**, make it the last one and say so
plainly and kindly: you won't write again about this, and the door stays open
("No os escribo más sobre esto; si en algún momento os encaja, aquí estoy.").

**Rules** (hard):

- **90 words at most** before the signature. Shorter is better — three or four
  short sentences.
- **No self-introduction and no re-pitch.** They have the first email right above.
- **No guilt or pressure.** Banned: "no he recibido respuesta", "solo quería hacer
  seguimiento", "por si se os pasó", "vuelvo a escribiros", "just checking in",
  "bumping this", "following up on my last email", any fake urgency.
- **Same voice as the first email**: plural `vosotros` in Spanish (never `usted`,
  never `tú`), first-person singular for the sender, "we" for the studio, hedged
  guesses rather than diagnoses, one task named.
- **Never name specific people, never mention the agent found them, no price,
  no hype, no exclamation marks.**
- **Plain text** — no links in the body; the signature carries them.
